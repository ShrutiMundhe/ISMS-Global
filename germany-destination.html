VISA GUIDE

German Student Visa
Application Process 2026

Follow the official roadmap to secure your National (D) Visa. We guide you through document collection, VFS booking, and blocked account setup.

Step-by-Step Visa & Admission Timeline

01

Secure Admission (Zulassungsbescheid)

Apply to German universities - your visa process can't start without this.

02

Apply for APS Certificate

Submit academic documents to the APS office at the German Embassy, New Delhi, as soon as possible - mandatory before booking your visa appointment.

03

Open a Blocked Account

Deposit 11,904 EUR (approx. ₹10,71,360) and get your confirmation certificate.

04

Complete CSP Application

Fill out the online form on digital.diplo.de and upload documents for embassy pre-screening.

05

Book VFS Appointment

Book at VFS centers (New Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, Chandigarh, Jalandhar, Goa and Kochi). Slots are highly competitive during peak season (May - August) and are available on a first-come-first-serve basis.

06

Attend VFS Appointment

You'll need originals of your visa and supporting documents, and a copy of both your visa and supporting documents. This should only take 15 - 30 minutes. You'll do some biometrics (scanning and fingerprints).

07

Pay Your Visa Fees

Once the biometrics have been completed, you'll have to go over to the window and pay your fee and the VFS fee. They charge you 75 EUR fee and then ₹1,800 - 2,500 in VFS charges. You will then have to wait 8 - 12 weeks for your visa!

08

Arrive & Complete Formalities

Register your address (Anmeldung) within 14 days, activate your blocked account, get health insurance, and apply for your residence permit.

ADMISSION REQUIREMENTS

What Are the Eligibility Requirements to Study in Germany?

Criteria Details
Academic Requirements Generally, 60%+ in your qualifying degree or 12th grade for public universities. Competitive programs (especially at TU9 technical universities) often expect 70%+ or equivalent strong CGPA.
Language Proficiency (English) For English-taught programs, IELTS 6.5+ or TOEFL 90+ is the benchmark.
Language Proficiency (German) For German-taught programs, Goethe-Zertifikat B2/C1 or TestDaF is required for academic eligibility.
Work Experience Not required for most Bachelor's or Master's admissions. However, relevant work experience (2+ years) is expected or strongly preferred for MBA programs and select DAAD scholarship tracks (like EPOS).

Study in Germany Without IELTS — Alternative Pathways

Yes, there is one possibility of studying in Germany without writing the IELTS exam: IF your prospective Germany based universities accept some alternate and authentic version of your proof of English proficiency level:

  • Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate: from your old English medium school/university - accepted by many universities instead of IELTS.
  • University Interview: In some cases, a direct alternative is an English test or interview administered by the university.
  • Alternative English Tests: TOEFL, PTE or Duolingo English Test - accepted by many universities according to institutional policy.
  • German-Taught Programs: if you have Goethe/TestDaF B2 German proficiency, you need not submit English scores at all.

* Always check with your shortlisted university on their specific waiver policy. This is institution by institution and not a blanket of national rule.

STUDENT LIFE

Life in Germany
for Indian Students

Finding suitable accommodation in Germany, especially in larger cities like Berlin, Munich and Frankfurt, is often a challenge for Indian students. There is a huge demand for housing in these cities. Indian students should know the top cities in Germany for international students to decide. The student accommodation in Germany is different in accordance with the budget and lifestyle of students coming from abroad. The most common include student dorms, shared flats and private rentals.

1. University Halls of Residence

Cost: around ₹19,800 - ₹21,600/month (220 - 240 EUR)

Most preferred and most affordable option; however, rooms are on first-come-first-serve basis - the university cannot guarantee you a place here. Begin looking through the university's International Office and/or DAAD Accommodation Finder from the summer semester onwards, even if your program starts in Winter.

2. Flat Sharing (WG)

Cost: Around ₹25,200/month (280 EUR; private market ranges from ₹27,000 to ₹58,500/month, approx. 300 - 650 EUR)

Two to three students live in an apartment, sharing rent and utilities - a convenient and more easily available option due to lack of halls space. However, prices will differ from city to city as the private market is not regulated. Look through wg-gesucht.de, studenten-wg.de, and wg-cast.de.

3. Private Apartment

Cost: Around ₹31,500/month (350 EUR; private market ranges from ₹45,000 to ₹81,000/month, approx. 500 - 900 EUR; private apartments in city centers average ₹74,500/month, approx. 828 EUR)

Preferable for PhD students or for those bringing their family (even some scholarships pay for spouse's accommodation). Find through immobilienscout24.de, immowelt.de, and immonet.de.

Housing Tip & Rent Costs Summary

Tip: Get at least temporary accommodation arranged before arrival - competition is fierce in all above-mentioned categories.

Part-Time Work Rules & Student Communities

International students in Germany are legally permitted to work part-time to support their education:

  • Students are allowed to work up to 140 days or 280 half-days in one year without any additional work permits.
  • Graduates receive an 18-month stay-back visa (job-seeking visa) after completing their studies, allowing them to work part-time while searching for a skilled job.
  • Student Communities: Connect with local Indian Student Associations (ISAs), student councils (AStA), and online forums (Facebook/Telegram groups like "Indians in Germany") for quick adaptation, community dinners, festival celebrations, and peer support.
