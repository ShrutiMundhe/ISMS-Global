WHY CHOOSE FRANCE?

Why Study in France? Key Reasons for Indian Students

France attracted over 400,000 international students in 2024-25. Here’s why you should consider France an option for your studies abroad:

2030 Goal

To enroll 30,000 Indian students in France annually by 2030 – an ambitious goal.

International Classes

A new program for 2024-2025 aimed at enabling students to come to France without speaking French. The focus is on complete immersion in language learning alongside conventional studies.

Young Professionals Scheme

Simplified routes for graduates to enter the French job market.

Globally recognised degrees and Grandes Écoles

French public and private institutions provide millions of internationally recognised degrees that are among the top in the world in terms of academic and research excellence. Additionally, students can submit their applications to Grandes Écoles, which are known institutions in business, engineering, public administration, and natural sciences that are highly valued by employers worldwide.

A wide range of English-taught programmes

French language proficiency isn’t always a requirement to study in French higher education institutions. In fact, you can choose from over 1,500 master’s programmes taught entirely in English, particularly in business, engineering, and IT.

Lower tuition fees compared to popular destinations

France offers comparatively lower tuition fees than countries like the UK and the US, especially at public universities where higher education is heavily subsidized by the French government.

High ROI with Programs Delivered in English

Now, there is no need to speak fluent French to get access to top-notch education. Currently, there are more than 1,600 fully English-taught programs.

Instant Access to Multinationals

France is home to 31 Fortune Global 500 companies. The graduates have direct access to companies like L’Oréal, Airbus, Capgemini, Michelin, and Sanofi. For instance, the Franco-Indian Roadmap project aims to accommodate 30,000 Indian students by 2030.

Post Study 5-Year Alumni Visa

France respects its alumni. You get a 5-year Short Stay Schengen Visa after your completion of your studies.

France Student Visa Process

What Are the Intakes in France?

What Are the Intakes in France?

France’s main academic intake starts in September (Fall) and has most of the academic courses and scholarship options. A second smaller intake is in January or February (Spring), and it is more limited to the selected types of business and engineering courses. The students need to start planning their applications 6 to 8 months before the academic semester begins to make sure that they have enough time for university evaluation and visa processing.

French Universities – Academic Entry Requirements

Every French university has its own academic entry requirements; therefore, you need to check your chosen course carefully. Admission standards differ depending on the programme levels, but the following overview will give you an idea of the entry requirements for all Indian students at different study levels.

Undergraduate Programmes - Entry Requirements

Most students require a finished secondary or high school education, the corresponding qualifications recognized by the university, good academic results, and relevant academic preparation in particular subjects, such as engineering, medicine, and architecture.

Completion of Class 12 from a recognized board with the minimum percentage required by the university (generally 55%–60%+). (Refer university-specific website)

Postgraduate Programmes - Entry Requirements

The standard requirements include the recognition of the bachelor's degree with academic transcripts, the relevant educational background, a curriculum vitae or resume, a Statement of Purpose, and letters of recommendation.

A recognized bachelor's degree in a relevant discipline with required aggregate marks. Some programmes, such as MBAs, may require 2–5 years of relevant work experience. (Refer university-specific website)

English-Taught Programmes

Proof of English proficiency via standardized tests like IELTS (typically 6.0–6.5+) or TOEFL iBT (80+). Some universities may accept Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificates. (Refer university-specific website)

French-Taught Programmes

Proof of French proficiency required through DELF/DALF exams (minimum B2 level for most courses) or TCF/TEF qualifying scores. (Refer university-specific website)

Campus France Registration

Mandatory Études en France (EEF) registration and academic interview process for students applying from eligible countries before visa application.

Financial & Visa Requirements

Proof of sufficient funds (minimum €615/month for living expenses), valid passports, flight itineraries, accommodation proof, and mandatory health insurance coverage.

What Are the Eligibility Criteria to Study in France Without IELTS?

  • Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate from your prior English-medium school/university — accepted by several institutions in place of IELTS.
  • University-administered English assessment or interview in lieu of a standardized test score.
  • Duolingo English Test (DET) or TOEFL as alternatives, depending on the institution's policy.
  • French-taught programs — if you meet DELF/DALF B2 French proficiency instead, English test scores aren't required at all.
  • Always verify the specific waiver policy directly with your shortlisted university/Grande École, as this varies significantly by institution.
  • Work experience (where applicable): Top MBA programs (HEC, ESSEC, ESCP) typically expect 2–5 years of relevant professional experience, along with GMAT/GRE scores, essays, and interviews — like MBA norms elsewhere in Europe.

Secrets for Searching for Student Housing in France

Acquiring the ideal student housing is reminiscent of playing darts and applying some measure of intuition. Although you will probably not find the apartments of your dream at the first opportunity, here are several tips to facilitate your study abroad experience:

Start 3-4 Months Early

Start searching for accommodation at least three to four months before your arrival, especially if you are going to a popular student city.

Define Your Budget

Know your budget, including the rent, utilities, food, and transportation expenses before starting your search for accommodation.

Prioritize Essentials

Think about what is most important for you, for instance, whether you want a furnished room or a room with attached bathroom facilities.

Check Listings Thoroughly

Check the listings thoroughly to know what the neighborhood is like, what facilities are nearby, how you can travel to and from your house, and what utilities are included.

Familiarize Extra Fees

Make sure to familiarize yourself with the fees and costs of living, including any agency or platform fees.

Use Trusted Services

Legal listings can be found on trusted websites and services.

Review Agreements

Be sure to read through the rental agreement and know everything about payments and fees before signing anything.

Work Regulations for Indian Students in Part-Time Jobs in France

If you decide to work while studying in France, you will appreciate the student-oriented work regulations of the country:

  • To be able to work, you should have a valid long-stay student visa (VLS-TS).
  • International students are authorized to work up to 964 hours per year (about 20 hours per week).
  • During official university holidays, students can work full time (up to 35 hours/week).
  • You don't need a special work permit.
  • Most employers provide a formal work contract, ensuring legal protection and minimum wage compliance.
  • As of 2026, students earn around €11.65 to €12 per hour (gross).
  • On average, students earn between €700 to €1,000 per month.
